Benchmark

The benchmark compares baseline (no cache) vs recycled (cache enabled) runs across TTFT, prefill throughput, end-to-end latency, cache hit rate, and output equivalence, then prints a composite score (0–100) with a letter grade.

toki bench   # interactive: pick a model from the registry + a workload from a menu

# Fully scripted (registry name or path):
toki bench --model 9b --workload agent_long_context \
           --max-tokens 64 --output-json report.json

# Self-contained: spin up llama-server + bench together (auto-stop)
toki bench --model ~/Downloads/Qwen3.5-9B-Q4_K_M.gguf \
           --self-contained --ctx-size 8192 --n-gpu-layers 99

Scoring

The composite score (0–100) is derived from weights that reflect production importance rather than end-to-end wall-clock:

ComponentWeightWhat it measures
Prefill skip30%% of prefill tokens served from cache
TTFT improvement25%Reduction in time to first token
Throughput gain20%Prefill tokens/sec improvement
Cache hit rate15%Hit rate
Output equivalence10%Semantic similarity of outputs
Wall-clock gain5%Total latency reduction

Letter grades: A+ ≥ 90, A ≥ 80, B ≥ 70, C ≥ 60, D ≥ 50, F < 50 (with A- / C- marking the lower end of a band).

Measured results (this hardware)

Run on an AMD Ryzen AI MAX+ 395 (Ryzen AI Halo), max-tokens=32. Disclaimer: all scores are based on multiple benchmark runs and depend on the hardware, environment, and model, the benchmark may score better or worse elsewhere.

The headline number is realistic_mixed: 201 prompts across 5 real-world categories (RAG, tool-using, multi-turn, support, one-off), TTFT cut by two thirds, prefill nearly 6x faster, 88% cache hit rate, composite 84.4 (A). The summarization and code_review workloads are even stronger (A+). Low scores on no_overlap and the adversarial workloads are expected, zero-overlap prompts are the worst case for prefix caching, and the stress workload is designed to surface edge cases (false-positive hits from cosine similarity, minimal savings on short prompts, underperforming sequential growth).

The 35B-A3B MoE benefits most from caching, its per-token compute is dominated by the 3B active experts, so skipping prefill shows up as larger relative TTFT and throughput gains than on the dense 9B (e.g. realistic_mixed 84.4 → 86.8 composite, summarization prefill speedup 1090% → 1187%). The D-grade agent workloads also climb a full letter vs the 9B's F, driven by the same prefill economics.

Across all four models the pattern holds: the bigger and sparser the model, the better the relative caching gains. The 122B-A10B tops every table (realistic_mixed 89.7, summarization 95.8, code_review 94.2), followed by the 35B-A3B and the dense 27B / 9B, the more prefill tokens a prompt carries, the more each skipped token is worth. no_overlap is a control workload (0% hit by design) and stress_adversarial deliberately maximizes false-positive hits and short-prompt edge cases, so its scores stay low on every model.
